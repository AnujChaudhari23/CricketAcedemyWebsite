# AI Defaults to Avoid

Why each look fails, when it is legitimate, and how to derive an alternative. The rule is "have a reason", not "never". The failure is that the look was chosen by habit, not by the audience.

Contents: 1 Cream + serif + terracotta · 2 Near-black + acid accent · 3 Broadsheet · 4 Numbered markers · 5 Gradient blobs · 6 Giant stat · 7 Glassmorphism · 8 Uniform grid · 9 Template chrome · 10 Overcorrection · 11 Derivation

## 1. Warm cream (~#F4F1EA) + high-contrast serif + terracotta (~#D97757)

- **Why it fails:** It is the current default "thoughtful brand" palette. A look that appears on every wellness, AI and newsletter page distinguishes none of them. **Note:** #D97757 is Anthropic's own Claude-interaction accent — on a user's brief it reads as a tell.
- **Legit when:** The brand already owns it, or the product is genuinely paper/print/food heritage and the palette comes from real materials.
- **Alternative:** Pull colour from the subject (a real photo, product, place, material). Pick type for the voice, not for "elegance".

## 2. Near-black + one acid-green/vermilion accent

- **Why it fails:** The dark-mode-SaaS template. One neon accent on black signals "developer tool" regardless of product.
- **Legit when:** Users work in dark environments (terminals, editing suites, trading).
- **Alternative:** Decide light/dark from usage context. Use two or three related hues with a clear role each.

## 3. Broadsheet layout, hairline rules, zero border-radius

- **Why it fails:** Borrows newspaper authority without newspaper content density.
- **Legit when:** Real editorial content that needs ruled structure.
- **Alternative:** Create structure with spacing, scale contrast and alignment.

## 4. Numbered markers (01/02/03) on non-sequential content

- **Why it fails:** Numbers promise order. When items are parallel, readers look for a sequence that isn't there.
- **Legit when:** Steps, ranked lists, chapters, timelines.
- **Alternative:** Meaningful labels, or no marker.

## 5. Gradient blobs

- **Why it fails:** Fill space without carrying meaning; cost paint time.
- **Legit when:** The brand's concept is fluid/atmospheric and the gradient has a defined role.
- **Alternative:** Real imagery, illustration with a point of view, or a purposeful pattern.

## 6. Giant stat + small label hero

- **Why it fails:** Reads as manufactured proof when the number isn't the page's core claim.
- **Legit when:** The number *is* the story and is verified.
- **Alternative:** Lead with the outcome in plain words, then support with evidence.

## 7. Glassmorphism cards without rationale

- **Why it fails:** Blur needs something worth seeing behind it. Over a flat background it lowers contrast.
- **Legit when:** Layers sit over rich imagery and AA contrast is verified.
- **Alternative:** Solid surfaces with a clear elevation system.

## 8. Uniform icon-title-paragraph feature grid

- **Why it fails:** Equal weight removes hierarchy; the reader can't find what matters.
- **Legit when:** Items are truly equal and scan-only.
- **Alternative:** Lead with the most important feature at larger scale; vary tile sizes.

## 9. Template chrome

A cluster of small tells that individually are fine but together shape the look of a generated page:

- Tracked-out ALL-CAPS eyebrow label above every heading
- Meta strings joined with middle dots (`A · B · C`)
- Labels built as `WORD — fragment` with a spaced em dash
- Tinted near-black (#0B0B0B, #111) standing in for black
- Monospace face for small data labels
- `→` appended to every link and button

**Why it fails:** Each is a stylistic choice applied unconditionally. Together they announce a template, not a brand.

**Alternative:** Pick one chrome device and use it for one purpose. Drop the rest.

## 10. Overcorrection

Avoiding all nine by drifting to the next most common choices (purple gradient, bento everywhere, serif-italic emphasis words) just changes the costume. Test: "Could this appear on three unrelated brands unchanged?" If yes, it's still a default.

## 11. Derive an alternative

1. Name the audience's emotional state on arrival.
2. Name the page's single job.
3. Find one concrete real-world source tied to the brand.
4. Derive palette, type voice and signature from 2 and 3.
5. Run the "three unrelated brands" test.
